01 · Positioning

Who to keep, who to court

The real customer mix, and the one line that sums up the pitch.

Positioning statement: "Golden's best-kept AYCE sushi secret — the fish quality beats every nearby rival; it's time more people found out."

The owner's read on the customer base is partly right. A real, vocal loyal base exists — "hidden gem," "our go-to," "been coming for years" outnumbers first-timer language 3.45 to 1, and satisfaction among people who do show up is high (4.48★, 72.7% five-star). Keep this group happy; they're not the problem.

The problem is who never hears about Uokura at all. Review volume is down 37% from its 2019 peak even though quality hasn't dropped — that's a discovery gap, not a satisfaction gap. Court new-visitor discovery: people searching "AYCE sushi Golden," tourists near Red Rocks/Coors/Mines, and neighbors who've simply never walked in.

The ask (free, ethical — not review-gating): after a good meal, a small table card or receipt line: "If we got it right, a Google review helps your neighbors find us." No discount, no incentive tied to leaving a review — that violates Google's policies, and the data doesn't show Uokura needs it. It shows Uokura needs to answer the reviews it already gets.

04 · Retention

Keep the regulars close

Low-cost systems that turn AYCE regulars into an actual list.

Email / SMS list capture

Low-cost

A paper sign-up card at the register or table — no POS integration needed to start. A free-tier tool (under ~500 contacts) covers a monthly "what's fresh this week" note.

Gift cards

Free — already have Clover

Uokura's existing POS can issue gift cards without new hardware. Promote at the holidays and alongside the private-party funnel below.

Birthday & regulars perk

Low-cost

Once the list above exists, a once-a-year birthday note with a small perk costs almost nothing and reads as genuinely neighborhood, not corporate.

AYCE punch card

Free — paper only

A simple stamp card (10th visit, one item free) rewards exactly the loyal base the review data already confirms is real — zero tech required.

05 · Revenue add-ons

Ask for the business that's already there

Private parties, boat platters, and gifting — demand that doesn't need new infrastructure.

Private parties & boat platters

An inquiry funnel on the new website (Session 08) — a simple form or email, no online payment required. Matches the locked scope: call-to-reserve, not live booking.

Catering inquiries

Same funnel, same page — office lunches and events are a natural extension of the AYCE format the restaurant already runs well.

Holiday gifting

Pair gift cards with a simple printed note card at the holidays — low effort, matches an occasion people are already buying for.

06 · Future initiative

Online ordering

Analysis only — not being built now. Drop this in when the owner's ready.

Recommendation: first-party, pickup-only, capped hours

When ready

The demand signal is real but small (19 of 788 reviews, 2.4%) and the documented failure is operational — an unanswered phone and a silent cancellation — not a missing DoorDash listing. Every nearby AYCE rival carries 3–7 marketplace integrations; Uokura is the only one sampled with zero online-ordering or reservation hook. Start with Clover Online Ordering, pickup only, during a defined 2–3 hour window. Hold off on delivery marketplaces — the 15–30% commission doesn't remove the packing-labor cost, which is the real constraint.
